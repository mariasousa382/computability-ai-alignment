# AI Alignment and the Halting Problem

A technical blog post and critique exploring the relationship between computability theory and AI alignment, based on the paper Machines that halt resolve the undecidability of artificial intelligence alignment by Melo et al. (2025).

This project explores the relationship between computability theory and AI alignment, focusing on why a universal verifier for AI alignment cannot exist and what architectural alternatives might still allow for provably aligned systems.

## Overview

The paper argues that determining whether an arbitrary AI system will always behave according to a desired alignment function is undecidable.

Using ideas from:

* Turing’s Halting Problem
* Rice’s Theorem
* Computability Theory
* AI Alignment Theory

the paper shows that no universal verifier can determine whether every possible AI system is aligned across all possible inputs.

This analysis explains the paper’s core arguments and critiques the practical feasibility of its proposed solutions.

## Topics Covered

* Inner vs outer alignment
* Turing machines and computability
* The Halting Problem
* Rice’s Theorem
* AI verification impossibility
* Architectural alignment guarantees
* Halting-constrained AI systems
* Agent loops and undecidability
* Constitutional AI and RLHF
* AI safety tradeoffs

## Why This Matters

As AI systems become increasingly autonomous and agentic, understanding the theoretical limits of alignment verification becomes more important.

This project explores how classical computability theory applies directly to modern AI safety problems.
